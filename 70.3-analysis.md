---

## 1. How this was produced

187 gzipped `.FIT` files were decoded to full resolution: every `session`, `lap`, `length` (per-25 m swim), and `record` (1 Hz HR / power / speed / altitude / distance) message. From that I derived his physiological thresholds from the data itself rather than trusting device settings, classified all 187 sessions, then had six independent reviewers audit each ~6-week block and correct the automated labels.

**Confidence note.** Everything in the tables below is measured, except the *intent* of each session — that is inferred. Where inference was shaky, the `conf` column says so. Four weeks contain zero data (2025-W50, 2026-W02, 2026-W14, 2026-W28); these are almost certainly real interruptions rather than export failures, because the weeks either side show cautious "return to training" patterns.

Two data traps worth knowing about, because they would mislead anyone reading his files casually:

- **His power data is two different things.** Cycling power (median 165 W) and Garmin *running* power (median 345 W) live in the same field on different scales. Pooled naively, they suggest a median of 235 W and an FTP far above reality.
- **He never once did a maximal test.** His best-ever 20 min bike power (222 W) came from a *2.5 h endurance ride* at HR 158 — comfortably sub-threshold. So every power-zone percentage in his files is computed against a 275 W FTP that his own data does not support, which makes easy rides look like tempo. Several sessions that appear to be "sweet-spot" work were, by HR, 89 % Zone 1.

## 6. Phase-by-phase, with what each block was actually for

**Base 1 · 2025-W44–W49 (Nov)** — Building a routine from near-zero to 6.7 h. Volume ramp was fine; intensity was not: weekly "hard" time ran 30–53 %. The real high-intensity work was on the bike (8 × 60 s at 103–115 % FTP), which was legitimate; the runs were accidentally hard. Ended with a deload where running vanished entirely, then a blank week.

**Base 2 · 2025-W51–2026-W05 (mid-Dec → early Feb)** — Holiday-disrupted, with a fully blank week (2026-W02). Bike work acquired real structure here: clean 2 × 9.7 min at 97 % FTP. Runs stayed continuous tempo with no discrete intervals. Two December runs show HR badly decoupled from pace (HR 170 avg at 6:18 /km, hitting the all-time max of 184 twice) — flagged as either strap noise or an early illness signal, notably sitting right before the blank week.

**Build 1 · 2026-W06–W11 (Feb → mid-Mar)** — The best-executed block. Volume 6.1 → 8.2 h with a stable template, alternating threshold-heavy and aerobic weeks, then a step-back. Genuine VO2max work appears for the first time (6 × 1 min at 3:50–4:30 /km, HR to 181). Volume peaked in W10 at 8.2 h delivered at only **7 % hard** — a textbook aerobic overload week.

**Build 2 · 2026-W12–W18 (late Mar → early May)** — Derailed. A blank week (W14) then a 1.8 h return week, followed by an **8.0 h week** — a +6.2 h single-week jump after a two-week interruption. That is the one pacing decision in the log I would call genuinely risky. The back half consolidated run threshold work and shifted toward run volume.

**Build 3 · 2026-W19–W24 (May → mid-Jun)** — The seam. Long rides grew to 2.6–2.74 h, then in W23 the plan pivoted: **first open-water swim (1 Jun) and first brick (6 Jun)** anywhere in the dataset. Peak volume 9.4 h in W24, well tolerated.

**Peak & taper · 2026-W25–W31 (late Jun → 31 Jul)** — Interrupted again (a 9-day blank), then compressed into the highest 2-week load of the entire export (W29–W30, 16.1 h), then a clear one-week taper: volume down 35–43 %, the brick dropped, one sharp interval run retained, and the final session in the whole dataset an easy **1.8 km open-water swim** — exactly race distance — on Friday 31 July.

**Inference: his race was within about a week of 1 August 2026.** The export ends the day after a race-distance open-water rehearsal. No swim+bike+run day exists anywhere in the files, so the race itself was never captured.

It is a well-constructed session. The criticism is only that it arrived in week 31 of 36.

## 8. Verdict — what to copy, what to fix

**Copy:**
1. **The fixed weekly skeleton.** Same sessions, same days, for nine months. It absorbed four interruptions without collapsing.
2. **~85 % easy from the base phase onward.** His fitness inflected exactly when his intensity dropped, not when it rose.
3. **Two quality sessions per week, maximum.** Never more, regardless of volume.
4. **Long ride progression to ~2.5 h** with genuinely easy HR.
5. **The brick design** — easy bike, then race-pace run segments.
6. **Race-distance open-water rehearsal** in the final week.
7. **A short, sharp taper** that cuts volume ~40 % but keeps one fast session.

**Fix:**
1. **The long run.** He never exceeded 70 min / 12.2 km, then intended to race 21.1 km. Build to 100–110 min.
2. **Start bricks far earlier** — week 16–18, not week 31.
3. **Don't start base phase at 46 % hard.** His own data shows the easy months were the productive ones.
4. **Test your thresholds.** He trained for nine months against a 275 W FTP that was ~15 % too high, so his "sweet-spot" rides were often Zone 1.
5. **Don't rebound 1.8 h → 8.0 h** after an interruption. Step back up over two weeks.
6. **Rebalance swim time.** 26 % of hours for 13 % of the race, at 60–90 min a session, is a poor exchange rate *once you can comfortably cover the distance*.
7. **Get the long ride to race distance.** 78 km max vs a 90 km race leg.

**Your confirmed priorities:** 8–10 hours/week, with occasional longer peak weeks; a weightlifting background, stronger in upper-body experience than lower-body experience; maintain upper-body strength, improve lower-body strength, and include one upper, one lower, one core, and one plyometric session each week. No current injuries reported. Plyometric experience is unconfirmed, so jumping starts conservatively.

**Endurance starting point from the original plan:** 10 km run / 50 km ride; not yet swimming 1.9 km continuously; HR strap and GPS watch, no power meter. These are starting assumptions, not recent test results.

The aim is to finish the 70.3 well while developing strength, power, balance, and movement skills. The weekly structure keeps two swims, two bikes, two runs, and your four gym sessions; short brick runs join later. Four gym sessions add about **two hours** in the main build. Initial adaptation, holiday, recovery, and taper weeks are shorter than your available budget. Peak scheduled training reaches **10 h 20 min**, before any extra time needed for a slower 90 km rehearsal.

The main changes are a protected Friday rest day, progressive lower-body lifting, short fresh plyometric sessions, and less endurance intensity. The old automatic Tuesday bike intervals and Wednesday run intervals are removed. Most endurance work stays conversational; one controlled effort block can be included when recovery allows.

## C2. Your four gym sessions

All gym time includes warm-up and between-set rest. A listed exercise is a movement slot: use a familiar, comfortable variation. Keep the main lifts stable for 4–6 weeks so progress is measurable.

### Upper — maintain strength, about 35 minutes

Warm up for approximately 5 minutes, then:

| Movement | Example | Working sets × reps |
|---|---|---|
| Horizontal push | Dumbbell or barbell bench press | 2 × 5–8 |
| Horizontal pull | Chest-supported row | 2 × 6–10 |
| Vertical pull | Pull-up or lat pulldown | 2 × 6–10 |
| Vertical push | Dumbbell overhead press or landmine press | 2 × 6–10 |

Use familiar loads with **2–3 good repetitions left in reserve**. Maintaining load and clean repetitions is the goal. Increase only if swimming and recovery remain good; there is no need to add sets every week. On shortened weeks use one set per movement, or two sets each of one push and one pull.

### Lower — the main strength progression, about 45 minutes

Warm up for 7–8 minutes, including gradual warm-up sets.

| Movement | Example | Working sets × reps once adapted |
|---|---|---|
| Squat | Goblet squat, front squat, or familiar back squat | 3 × 5–8 |
| Hip hinge | Romanian deadlift | 2 × 6–8 |
| Single-leg strength | Supported split squat or step-up | 2 × 6–8 each leg |
| Calf strength | Controlled calf raise | 2 × 10–15 |

Rest around 2 minutes between demanding sets; take longer if needed and drop a final accessory set to stay within the time budget.

- **Weeks 1–2:** 1–2 light working sets per movement; leave 3–4 repetitions in reserve. Learn how the legs respond alongside running.
- **Week 3:** build toward two sets per movement. Week 4 is lighter.
- **Weeks 5–15:** use the full prescription when tolerated. Once every set reaches the top of its range with 2–3 repetitions still available, add the smallest practical load next time.
- **Weeks 17–22:** maintain strength with 1–2 sets per movement and familiar loads; stop chasing personal records. Recovery and rehearsal weeks use the shorter allowance in C3.
- **Week 23:** light familiar movements only, around half the normal sets. **Week 24:** no loaded lower session.

Progress one lifting variable at a time: load, repetitions, or sets. Hold jump volume steady when the long run grows, and add weight only if running and recovery still feel normal. When introducing a new jump drill, hold lifting load steady. If Tuesday leaves you sore enough to change Wednesday's running mechanics, replace that run with an easy walk or rest and reduce the next lower session.

### Core — control and movement, about 20 minutes

After a short warm-up, complete two controlled rounds:

| Exercise | Dose per round |
|---|---|
| Dead bug | 6–8 each side |
| Side plank | 20–30 seconds each side |
| Pallof press | 8–10 each side |
| Suitcase carry | 20–30 metres each side |

Rest as needed and keep breathing normally. For variety, a light controlled cable chop can replace the Pallof press for a training block. On 10–15-minute weeks, do one round or omit the carry. This session supports trunk control; it should not exhaust you for swimming or running.

### Plyometrics — fresh power and landing skill, 10–20 minutes

Warm up with brisk walking or easy cycling, ankle movements, and controlled squats. Use a stable, non-slip surface and stop while jumps remain crisp.

- **Weeks 1–2:** landing-position practice plus, if comfortable, **2 × 3 small two-foot jumps**, resetting between repetitions. The session is mostly preparation.
- **Weeks 3–15:** build toward **3 × 3 low countermovement jumps + 2 × 5 low ankle hops**, about 19 landing events. Rest 60–120 seconds between sets; reset between jumps as needed.
- Once these are comfortable, small lateral jumps **2 × 3 in each direction** may replace the countermovement jumps for a block. They are a substitute, not an added exercise.
- **Ease weeks and weeks 17–22:** roughly halve the jumping sets; keep the warm-up.
- **Weeks 23–24:** omit plyometrics.

One two-foot landing counts as one landing event here. Avoid weighted jumps, depth jumps, and fatigue circuits in this plan. Stop if landings become noisy or unstable, or pain appears. If your legs have not recovered from Tuesday or Wednesday, use the slot for balance and mobility instead of jumping.

The emphasis on a few exercises and low repetition counts follows [NSCA guidance on plyometric programming](https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/plyometric-exercises/). The once-weekly frequency and conservative starting dose are choices for your combined schedule, not a claim that this is the optimal power-only program.

## C4. Endurance progression and milestones

### Swimming stays a priority

Monday emphasizes technique; Thursday emphasizes comfortable continuous swimming. Include drills and easy swimming within the scheduled minutes.

| Weeks | Target progression |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | Comfortable 25–100 m repeats with rest; breathing and body position first |
| 5–8 | Progress toward 200–600 m continuous swimming |
| 9–12 | Progress toward 800–1,000 m continuous |
| 13–16 | Progress from 1,200–1,500 m toward **1,900 m continuous** |
| 17–22 | Consolidate 1.9 km; practice sighting, wetsuit use, and open water where feasible |

These are readiness targets, not deadlines to force. Repeat a stage if form or breathing deteriorates. A few coached technique sessions early would be useful. Allow the longer Thursday slot for the 1.9 km milestone; extend the time budget if your comfortable pace requires it.

Aim for at least two supported open-water practices before the race when conditions permit. Use a supervised venue or capable buddy and conditions suitable for your experience; winter cold-water exposure is not a training requirement. Pool sighting and continuous swimming help, but do not reproduce all open-water demands.

### Bike and run

Tuesday's bike and Wednesday's run are conversational, around **2–4 out of 10 perceived effort**. Hills and wind change pace; slow down to preserve the intended effort. The long run grows toward 110 minutes only while recovery remains good.

- **Weeks 1–8:** easy endurance while the new weekly load settles. No mandatory maximal tests.
- **Weeks 9–15:** if recovering well, include up to **2 × 8–10 minutes of controlled steady riding** within Saturday's ride, with 5 easy minutes between. Otherwise keep it all easy.
- **Weeks 17–22:** on suitable weeks, use **2 × 15–20 minutes at intended sustainable 70.3 bike effort** within the long ride. Alternatively practice brief race-effort segments in the brick. Choose one focus that week; do not add hard work to both.
- **Weeks 23–24:** much shorter sessions, with only brief familiar pickups.

This deliberately trades some threshold training for lower-body strength and power development. Keep at least **85–90% of endurance minutes easy**; there is no quota of hard minutes to fill. That percentage excludes lifting and jumping, which still count toward fatigue.

### Bricks and rehearsal

The first bricks in weeks 10–11 are easy 10–15-minute runs after the bike. Progress using C3, reaching 25–30 minutes later. Change shoes and begin at a comfortable effort; the purpose is learning the transition, not creating another interval workout.

In week 21, rehearse kit, breakfast, fueling, and pacing with a **90 km ride plus up to 30 minutes of easy running**, provided the preceding weeks support it. A comfortable 1.9 km swim can precede it if the logistics and recovery make sense; move the scheduled Thursday swim to Saturday rather than adding a third swim. It is also reasonable to swim separately. This rehearsal replaces that week's harder endurance work.

### Measure progress without borrowing another athlete's zones

In weeks 1, 9, and 17, record pace or distance and HR during comparable easy sessions, plus swim distance and the main lifting loads. Look for a trend at similar effort and conditions.

Formal threshold testing is optional, once familiar with the protocol and recovered. A test replaces a scheduled effort session; reduce lower-body lifting and omit jumps that week if necessary. Do not stack three maximal tests onto a full gym week.

If using a 400 m / 200 m swim test once both distances are comfortable, the correct CSS calculation is **(400 m time − 200 m time) ÷ 2**, with both times in seconds, giving **seconds per 100 m**. Run and bike HR zones need their own assessment; the other athlete's HR numbers and the old plan's generic percentages are not your validated zones.

## C5. Recovery, flexibility, and race preparation

- **Keep the four gym focuses in normal weeks, but scale the dose.** They are reduced during recovery weeks and intentionally omitted in part during the taper.
- **Use the full rest day.** Move a missed workout only if it fits without creating a string of demanding leg days. Otherwise skip it.
- **If the gym and endurance goals compete, keep upper maintenance, reduce optional accessories and endurance intensity, then shorten lower-body sets or omit jumps as needed.** Protect swimming practice and a sustainable progression of the long ride/run; no workout is mandatory through pain or excessive fatigue.
- **After an interruption, resume below the last completed load.** Repeat a previously comfortable week and build back according to recovery; do not catch up missed volume.
- **Repeated poor sleep, unusual fatigue, or declining performance calls for an easier week.** Persistent or localized pain, especially if it changes movement, calls for stopping the provoking exercise and assessment.
- **Versatility comes from the movement mix:** push, pull, squat, hinge, single-leg work, trunk control, carries, jumping, and lateral movement. Swap equivalent exercises or use an indoor trainer when useful; avoid adding random hard circuits on top.
- **Fuel doubles and long sessions.** Eat between sessions. Practice a tolerable carbohydrate intake on long rides, working toward roughly 60–90 g/hour if tolerated; adjust fluids to conditions. Use the rehearsed approach on race day.

For **24 April 2027**, aim for a calm 1.9 km swim, a sustainable 90 km bike, and a controlled start to the 21.1 km run. A comfortable finish takes priority over a predicted finish time. The revised schedule does not assume the other athlete's pace, power, or race performance.
